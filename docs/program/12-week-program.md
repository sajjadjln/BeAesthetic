## 0. What changed, and why the program is different

| Before (Jul 11 block) | Now | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Damavand Aug 11 was the backbone | **No event.** Goal = proportional, strong, healthy body; shoulders + arms priority | Legs stop being "mountain insurance" and become physique + knee-health work. Descent-armor block retired. |
| 5 training days + 2 runs + weekly loaded hike | **4 lifting days**, optional easy cardio | The concurrent-training penalty largely lifts (see §1) |
| Knees = watch item under heavy eccentric descent load | **Post-Damavand knee episode, now resolved**; 3 weeks fully off | Legs re-enter from an earlier rung (§11.4), not from where Block 1 stopped |
| Beginner low-end volume (4–6 sets/muscle) | **Mid-bracket 6–8 · priority muscles 8–10** | The single biggest upgrade in this program — see §1 |

### §1 — The volume upgrade (the important one)

Decision Guide §4 puts a beginner at **4–8 hard sets per muscle per week**, minus **20–30% for concurrent running/hiking/climbing**. In the last block you were running twice and hiking a loaded mountain every week, so you trained at the **bottom** of that range (~4–6 sets) — correctly, because endurance work and lifting spend the same recovery budget (§5.2, §6).

That endurance load is now gone. **Removing it doesn't just free up days, it raises the ceiling on how much lifting you can recover from.** So this block sits at **6–8 sets/muscle/week baseline, 8–10 for shoulders and arms** (Decision Guide §4.4: a lagging/priority muscle gets +4–6 weekly sets while everything else holds at maintenance). Roughly **50–70% more stimulus for the exact muscles you care about**, at the same 4 sessions.

That is what you're actually buying by not training for a mountain. Worth naming, because in ~6 weeks you'll be tempted to add running back in — and if you do, the volume above has to come down again (§4.5).

### §2 — Knee re-entry rule (non-negotiable)

You injured it, it resolved, you did nothing for 3 weeks. Decision Guide §11.4: **resume from an earlier ladder step, not where you left off.** So:

- **Weeks 1–2:** two-legged and isometric only — wall sit / Spanish squat, glute bridge, bodyweight split squat, calf raise. No step-downs, no Bulgarians, no load.
- **Week 3:** add backpack split squat (light) if weeks 1–2 were fully pain-free, including next-day.
- **Week 5:** Bulgarian split squat re-enters, bodyweight first.
- **Week 7+:** eccentric step-downs return as maintenance only, never as a priority lift again this block.

**Why eccentric work is the treatment, not the danger.** Walking downhill is almost pure eccentric quad loading — the muscle lengthens under load to absorb your bodyweight plus pack, thousands of times. When the quad runs out of capacity it stops absorbing that force, and the force goes into the joint instead. That is what happened to you. So the fix is not avoiding eccentric load, it is *building tolerance to it slowly* — which is exactly what the ladder above does, and why step-downs come back in Week 7 rather than never.

**Stop rule:** joint pain *during* a loaded range, or knee soreness that is still there the next morning → drop back one step and hold it two weeks (§11.1–11.4). Muscle burn and normal DOMS are not that. Tell me either way — this decides the next week's prescription.

## 3. The Weekly Split — 4 days

| Day | Session | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Sat** | **Upper A — Pull + Shoulders** | 50–60 min |
| **Sun** | *(optional)* easy 20–30′ walk/jog or pool | — |
| **Mon** | **Lower + Core** | 35–45 min |
| **Tue** | *(optional)* easy 20–30′ walk/jog or pool | — |
| **Wed** | **Upper B — Push + Arms** | 45–55 min |
| **Thu** | **REST — protected. Nothing.** | — |
| **Fri** | **Upper C — Shoulders/Arms + BFR**, then **Lower B — knee & descent capacity (15′)** | 45–55 min |

**Why this shape:**
- All four sessions are on days you're free in the morning (Sat/Mon/Wed/Fri), so no more evening lifts and no more caffeine-cutoff problem. 48 h between every session.
- **Legs are trained twice a week: Monday (full day) and Friday (Lower B, 15 min at the end of the arm session).** This was upgraded on 2026-09-06 after you identified the descent as where the knee went. Friday's block is no longer maintenance — it is a second real dose of exactly the quality that failed on the mountain: eccentric quad capacity, isometric knee loading, and ankle work. It sits *after* the arm work so the session still opens with what you like, and it is short enough that it never becomes "leg day twice."
- Shoulders get hit **three times** (press Sat, indirect Wed, laterals Sat + Fri), arms twice hard — that's how the 8–10 priority sets get distributed without any single session becoming junk volume (§1.2: cap ~8–10 hard sets/muscle/session).
- **Cardio is optional and easy.** Two 20–30′ walks, jogs, or swims for health, blood pressure and recovery — not training. If you start running seriously again, tell me and I cut lifting volume per §4.5.

### FRI (second half) — Lower B: Knee & Descent Capacity (15′)

*Added 2026-09-06. This is the block that fixes the thing that broke. It scales by week — do the row for the week you're in.*

| Wk 1–2 | Wk 3–6 | Wk 7–12 |
|---|---|---|
| Wall sit 2 × 30–45″ | Wall sit / Spanish squat 2 × 45″ | **Eccentric step-down 3 × 8/leg @ 3–5″ lowering** |
| Knee-to-wall ankle mobilisation 2 × 10/side | Backpack split squat 2 × 10/leg | Backpack split squat 2 × 10/leg |
| Single-leg calf raise 2 × 15 | Single-leg calf raise 2 × 15–20 | Single-leg calf raise 3 × 15–20 |
| Tibialis raise (toes up, back to wall) 2 × 15 | Tibialis raise 2 × 20 | Tibialis raise 2 × 20 |

**Rules:** RPE 7 — this is capacity work, not a burnout. Eccentric step-downs are the only movement in this program that gets a hard progression gate: **three consecutive pain-free weeks at the current step height before you raise it.** Control the lowering; if you drop into the bottom, the step is too high.

**Why tibialis and calf work is in here:** the muscles that control your ankle are the first line of shock absorption on a descent. Stiff, weak ankles land you stiff-legged and send the impact straight up to the knee. Two minutes a week, and they're the cheapest knee insurance you own.

**BFR starts Week 3, not Week 1.** It is a to-failure technique and Weeks 1–2 are re-entry; stacking a novel high-fatigue method onto detrained arms is how a re-entry week becomes a sore, skipped one (§6.2: intensity techniques carry a real fatigue cost and are restricted to isolation work). It is also better spent later — it arrives as the new thing just as the first novelty of the block wears off. **Weeks 1–2 substitute:** DB curl 2 × 15 and overhead triceps extension 2 × 12 at RPE 8.

**BFR rules (§4.4):** tight enough to restrict veins, never numbness or a white limb; remove immediately on sharp pain. This is the evidence-backed answer to 8.5 kg dumbbells — light-load BFR grows muscle comparably to heavy work. It is also your fastest visible feedback: the pump is real and immediate.

## 7. Nutrition — the actual limiter now

You are lean, you want size, and you just spent 3 weeks not training after a 5,610 m expedition. **Phase: lean gain** (§7.2) — but re-check the scale first: post-Damavand plus three weeks off, you may be *under* 63 kg.

| Target | Number | Practically |
|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | **110–130 g/day** (1.6–2.2 g/kg, §7.3) | Breakfast 3 eggs + yogurt (~25–30 g) · lunch palm of chicken/meat + rice or pasta (~30–35 g) · snack yogurt/milk + nuts (~20 g) · dinner meat or lentils (~30–35 g) |
| **Energy** | **+5–10% over maintenance (~+150–300 kcal)** | A glass of milk + a handful of nuts daily. **Not more** — a bigger surplus on a lean lifter is mostly fat (§10.3) |
| **Rate check** | **+0.15–0.3 kg/week** | Weigh Saturday AM, after bathroom, before food. Faster than this = fat, trim the add-ons (§10.4) |
| **Water** | **2.5–3 L/day** | Still the cheapest win available to you. Baseline was ~1 L |
| **Expectation** | **+1.5–2 kg over 12 weeks, mostly lean** | Beginner rate ~1–2 lb/month (§7.6). Visible change is **quarterly, not weekly** — this is exactly the belief that has ended your previous attempts |

**Supplements:** creatine 3–5 g/day, every day, no loading — the single highest-value purchase for this goal (§10.7). Protein powder only as convenience to hit the gram target. Nothing else is needed.

**Blunt version:** at 4 lifting days and no endurance load, your training is no longer the limiting factor. **Protein and the scale are.** If 12 weeks from now the tape hasn't moved, the first thing I will audit is not your program — it's whether you ate.

## 9. Mobility — 5 minutes daily *(revised 2026-09-06: ankle-led)*

You named mobility as half of your weak link, and for descending you're right — but the joint that matters most isn't the one people stretch. **Ankle dorsiflexion is the limiter.** If your shin can't travel forward over your foot, you land on a straight leg, and a straight leg has no shock absorption: the impact goes past the muscle and into the knee. Two of the old Daily 5 have been swapped out for it.

**The Daily 5 (evening, or right after training):**

1. **Knee-to-wall ankle mobilisation — 10 slow reps + 30″ hold per side** *(new, and the most important item on this list)*
2. **Calf + soleus stretch — 45″/side** *(new: straight-leg for calf, bent-knee for soleus)*
3. Deep squat hold, heels down — 45″
4. Hamstring stretch — 60″/side *(the toe-touch project — we measure it)*
5. Quad + hip-flexor stretch — 45″/side

*Dropped:* 90/90 hip and doorway pec. The pec stretch lives in your push-day warm-up now, so nothing is actually lost.

**We measure this one too.** Knee-to-wall test: kneel with your toes a measured distance from a wall, drive the knee forward keeping the heel down, and record the largest gap in cm at which the knee still touches. Baseline it in Week 1 alongside the toe touch. Typical adequate range is ~10 cm; below that is a genuine descent liability. This is the second number in the file that turns "my mobility is weak" from a feeling into something that can be shown to improve.

Static stretching goes after training or in the evening (§9.2). It buys mobility, not muscle (§9.4) — growth lives in the loaded stretch positions.

## 10. Recovery Rules

1. **Thursday is sacred.** Zero training.
2. **Sleep 11 pm – 6:30 am**, consistent. It is a training variable (§8.1).
3. **Water 2.5–3 L/day.**
4. **No cold showers/plunges right after lifting** — post-lift cold blunts growth (§8.3).
5. **Pool 1×/week** if you want it — active recovery you'll actually do (§8.2).
6. **Smoking:** you quit 2026-07-04 and then climbed Damavand. That streak is the most valuable asset in this file.
7. **Minimum viable session** = warm-up + first two exercises. On a bad day, do that and leave. It counts and it gets logged.

## 11. Substitutions & Contingencies

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|

| Barbell plates / dip bars bought | Tell me; it rewrites Mon and Wed |
| Missed a day | It's gone. Don't stack make-ups. Resume the template, never "restart" |
