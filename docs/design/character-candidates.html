Drawn in the style of your three references: heavy, rough ink lines, a cold blue palette with a glow behind the figure, fog that swallows everything below the waist, and dry, deadpan faces. The hoopoe stays and gets the same treatment, next to the new wizard and the goth lady.

Two changes from the references, on purpose. Their red spot color is your accent here (the app never uses red), so it lights the wizard's staff, the lady's pendant and the hoopoe's sprout. And the lady wears a high lace collar and long sleeves, which is very goth and keeps the app safe for Cafe Bazaar later.

What this style brings with it

A deadpan face is fine; a judging face is not

The references' charm is the unimpressed look, and the no-shame rule (D84) still holds. So the face stays deadpan as a personality, the words are always on your side, and nobody ever looks at you like that after a gap. Dry praise lands harder than cheering: بد نبود. from her means something.

The app turns cooler and darker

These panels are night-blue. The dark theme (slate, D136) takes them well. On the light theme they become dark tiles, which still works, but the whole app may want to move toward blue and fog to match.

Still vector, still buildable

Same as round 2: drawings in code, sharp at any size, small in the APK, tinted from the accent. The rough ink comes from one filter, so it costs nothing per pose. About 4–6 states per character.

My take

In this style, the goth lady has the most personality and the hoopoe is the strongest brand. She's funny, fresh and unlike any other habit app, and her dry lines fight boredom, your biggest risk. But she is the one most likely to drift into "unimpressed with you". The hoopoe is the safe, distinctive pick: Persian roots, a mark that reads at 24 px, and the deadpan eye makes him funnier than before. The wizard is well drawn but the most familiar of the three. My order: hoopoe, then the goth lady, then the wizard.
